How to write in

Write-in candidates aren't printed on the ballot, so you add the name yourself. On your Washington ballot:

  1. 1

    Find the race

    Locate U.S. Representative, Congressional District 7.

  2. 2

    Write the name

    On the blank write-in line, clearly print Peter Absolom Mbatia.

  3. 3

    Mark it

    Fill in the oval next to the write-in line, as your ballot's instructions show.

  4. 4

    Return it by Nov 3

    Put your ballot in a drop box by 8 p.m. or mail it postmarked by Election Day, November 3, 2026.

Washington write-in rules

  • A write-in candidate must file a Declaration of Write-in Candidacy by 8 p.m. on Election Day for their votes to be counted (RCW 29A.24.311).
  • Write-in candidates in the general election don't appear on the ballot, on the state website, or in the Voters' Pamphlet.
  • Write-in votes for declared candidates are only tallied by name if the total write-in vote in a race is high enough to change the outcome (RCW 29A.60.021).

About Peter

Why I'm Running

I'll be honest: getting in this late doesn't help my chances. But since Thanksgiving last year I've been on what I call the God Bless America Tour. It started as a thank-you to America. This country has been extraordinarily generous to immigrants. Immigrating here saves people from terrible poverty. I saw that poverty again when I went back to Africa about a year ago, where some people still don't get a meal every day. I wanted to start a conversation between immigrants and the people they live among, because too much has been lost between the two communities.

Then the war with Iran began, and everything changed. In my view, the war isn't going the way Americans were promised, and it is weighing on the economy that brings people to this country. Now it isn't only God bless America. It's God save America. Too many bad decisions are being made, and you can't change government unless you step up and stand in front of it.

The people of this district have trusted an immigrant to represent them in Congress. I'm an immigrant too, and I believe many immigrants want representation that knows their everyday struggles firsthand. That's why I'm putting my name in: to offer this district a real alternative to the status quo.

The A.I. Candidate

Let me be clear first: I am not a candidate created by AI. I'm a real person, a candidate who is using AI to run for office.

It's about getting big money out of politics. A candidate like me would normally never be a serious contender for Congress, and the first reason is money. Big money and special interests have taken over our politics. The people who can write big checks get first priority before everyday voters do. That's why so many people feel the government doesn't represent them.

Instead of hiring staff, secretaries and clerks, I'm running this campaign with AI agents doing the work. I believe I can run an effective campaign for less than $10,000 to $20,000, maybe one-hundredth of what other candidates spend. If that works, it changes everything. It means anyone can run, and voters get their freedom and independence back.

It's about facing AI without fear. My background is in computer science, and I think I understand this technology better than most. Some people believe AI will one day wake up and destroy us. I don't. AI is here to help. AI is here to level the playing field, opening doors that used to belong only to the few who had money and connections. That future is coming, and I want Washington to meet it with hope instead of fear.

Priorities

Housing

Summary: Too many people in this district are struggling to find a place to live and pay the rent. I'm running for the renters and middle-class families I live and work among, who are being priced out of the city they keep running.

What I'll do:

  • Be a voice in Congress for renters and the middle class, the people I live and work among, not the top earners.
  • Expand the Low-Income Housing Tax Credit and create a matching incentive for middle-income ("missing middle") homes so more housing gets built for working families.
  • Reward cities and counties that cut zoning barriers and permit delays with federal infrastructure and housing grants.
  • Expand Housing Choice Vouchers and keep emergency rental assistance available so a lost paycheck doesn't become an eviction.
  • Back down-payment help for first-time and first-generation homebuyers.
  • Push for new building technology, including modular and factory-built homes and AI-driven design, to bring down the cost of construction.

Cost of Living

Summary: Families are working long hours and still struggling to put food on the table. I believe a costly war, big money and a few companies controlling whole industries are squeezing everyday Americans, and new technology can make life's necessities affordable again.

What I'll do:

  • End the war, which in my view isn't going the way Americans were promised and is weighing on our economy.
  • Take on the "cartels," the few companies that control entire industries and push up prices, with tougher antitrust enforcement.
  • Help freelancers, small businesses and people with few resources use AI to start companies that compete with big corporations.
  • Protect SNAP and school-meal programs so no family in this country goes hungry.
  • Lower health-care costs by expanding Medicare drug-price negotiation and capping out-of-pocket insulin costs for everyone.
  • Expand the Child Tax Credit and support affordable child care for working parents.

Immigration

Summary: America has been extraordinarily generous to immigrants, and immigrating here saves people from terrible poverty. Our immigration system should stay open to ordinary, hard-working people, not only the rich and powerful, and immigrant communities deserve representatives who know their everyday struggles.

What I'll do:

  • Keep legal immigration open to ordinary people, not only the children of the rich and powerful.
  • Protect the Diversity Visa program, created in the Immigration Act of 1990, which opened America to people who could never have bought their way in.
  • Give immigrant communities representation that reflects everyday immigrants: the workers, the renters and the people sending money home.
  • Continue "Get to Know Your Local Immigrant" conversations to rebuild trust between immigrants and their neighbors.
  • Make sure foreign aid reaches the people it's meant for instead of the powerful, a lesson I saw Peace Corps volunteers learn in Africa in the late 1990s.
  • Lower the fees immigrants pay to send money home to their families.
  • Fund more immigration judges and caseworkers to cut green-card, visa and asylum backlogs.
  • Back a secure, orderly border alongside an earned path to legal status for Dreamers and long-time residents who work and pay taxes.

Money out of politics. Big donors and special interests get first priority in Washington, D.C. I'm running this campaign on AI agents instead of paid staff to show that a serious race can be run for less than $10,000 to $20,000, so that candidates answer to voters, not donors.

AI as an equalizer. AI is opening doors for people who were always shut out, people like me. I want Washington to meet it with hope instead of fear and to make sure its benefits reach everyone, not just the few at the top.

Peace and diplomacy. It's easy to start a war, but you can't end it alone. I'll push to end the war and rebuild America's alliances through conversation and respect for other nations' cultures, faiths and independence, not bombs and walls.

The Choice in This Race

My opponent on the ballot is Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal. She has represented this district for years, and I respect that the people here chose an immigrant to speak for them. I'm running because I believe that representation has drifted away from the everyday immigrants and working families of the 7th District. I mean the people working long hours, sending money home, struggling with rent, and quietly hoping someone in Washington, D.C. understands their lives.

Ilhan Omar of Minnesota and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani are not on this ballot. But they are examples of a style of politics I'm running against. Like me, both have roots in East Africa: Omar in Somalia, Mamdani in Uganda, and me in Kenya. That shared region is exactly why our differences matter. I believe their brand of politics speaks the language of activism more than the language of the immigrant who is simply trying to work, pay the bills and support family. It pushes positions that I think divide Americans and alienate the rest of the world.

I offer something different:

  • Lived experience, not distance. I don't live and work among the top earners. I live and work among the middle class.
  • Independence, not party machinery. I answer to voters, not to a party or to big donors.
  • Moderation, not extremes. I stand with reasonable Americans who respect each other and respect the world.
  • A clean campaign. No big money, just a real person using new tools to give the seat back to the people.

Why Independent

I was more of a Republican. I come from a very conservative part of the world, and many immigrants hold on to the conservatism of their culture and faith. But the current Republican leadership has taken turns I can no longer accept. I'm one of many Republicans jumping out of the boat. The war was the last straw. In my view, Americans haven't been told the truth about how the war or the economy is going. "Make America Great Again" has brought us decline.

I'm not committed to the Democratic Party either. I think its "woke" extremes push the rest of the world away and create more conflict than alliances. An immigrant's purpose is to build deep understanding between America and the rest of the world. I stand with the more moderate, more reasonable Americans who respect the world.

Tough Questions and Answers

Q: You've never served in government. Why should we trust you with a seat in Congress?

A: That's true. I've never held office, and I won't pretend otherwise. But I've kept myself clean. I'm glad I didn't go into politics earlier, because too often politics has been about money, and people sell their soul for it. My song for this campaign says it best: My clothes are old, but not dirty. I may not be rich, but I have my values and my self-respect. A clean system won't be built by the people who made it dirty.

Q: You trained in computer science but never worked in the field. Why not?

A: Getting in came down to who you know and who your people are, and as an immigrant I didn't score well on that. Climbing the corporate ladder asked more of me than I was willing to give. So I freelance, and I waited patiently for this moment. Today a person with very few resources can use AI to build something that competes with huge corporations.

Q: Why are you getting in so late?

A: Getting in this late doesn't help my chances, and I know it. But the war changed everything. God bless America became God save America, and you can't change government unless you step up.

Q: Is "The A.I. Candidate" an AI?

A: No. I'm a real person using AI to run a low-cost campaign, with AI agents in place of paid staff, so that big money doesn't decide who gets to run.

Q: Isn't AI dangerous?

A: I don't believe AI is going to wake up and destroy us. It's here to help and to level the playing field. Fear of AI mostly protects those at the top who already have access to everything.
